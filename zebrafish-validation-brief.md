# Zebrafish PI discussion brief

## Proposed discussion

We are reanalysing a public multi-tissue ocean-acidification RNA-seq dataset
from the coral reef fish *Acanthochromis polyacanthus*, focusing on
transposable-element expression. No archived damselfish RNA or tissue is
available. We would like to ask whether a small zebrafish study could test
either:

1. conservation of a selected TE-associated stress response; or
2. a candidate regulatory relationship between a TE-derived sequence and a
   nearby stress-response gene.

The zebrafish experiment would be described as cross-species functional
follow-up, not validation of intergenerational acclimation in damselfish.

## Information to bring to the PI

For each candidate:

- TE class, family, consensus sequence, and candidate locus;
- damselfish tissue, contrast, effect size, FDR, and family-line consistency;
- unique and multimapping read support;
- evidence against host-gene read-through;
- nearby gene and conserved synteny, if any;
- sequence similarity to the zebrafish genome or a zebrafish TE family;
- smallest assay that can answer a defined question.

Do not nominate candidates until the full computational analysis and
robustness checks are complete.

## Tier 1 — Low-burden molecular follow-up

### Question

Does a homologous zebrafish TE family or TE-associated gene respond to an
experiment already approved and routinely performed in the PI's laboratory?

### Possible material

- existing control and stress-exposed zebrafish RNA;
- existing cDNA from relevant tissues or developmental stages;
- no new animal exposure solely for this project, unless independently
  justified and approved.

### Assay

- DNase-treated RNA;
- strand-aware RT-qPCR or RT-ddPCR;
- at least two stable reference genes;
- no-template and no-RT controls;
- primer-efficiency testing;
- a single verified amplicon;
- Sanger confirmation of the amplicon.

This tier tests cross-species responsiveness. It does not establish a conserved
OA or intergenerational mechanism.

## Tier 2 — Transcript-origin validation

For the strongest conserved candidate:

- strand-specific RT-PCR;
- 5-prime or 3-prime RACE;
- targeted long-read cDNA sequencing if available.

This can distinguish an autonomous or TE-initiated transcript from intronic
signal or read-through.

## Tier 3 — Regulatory mechanism

Only if sequence and genomic context support a specific hypothesis:

- clone candidate TE-derived sequence upstream of a minimal promoter;
- compare reference, orientation-reversed, and motif-mutated constructs;
- test in an appropriate zebrafish cell or embryo assay;
- consider CRISPR interference or targeted deletion only with strong
  preliminary evidence and suitable laboratory expertise.

A reporter assay demonstrates regulatory potential outside the native locus.
Native-locus perturbation is required for a causal endogenous claim.

## Design cautions

- Damselfish and zebrafish TE families may not be one-to-one orthologues.
- A conserved nearby host gene does not imply a conserved TE insertion.
- Zebrafish validation should follow sequence and synteny analysis.
- Technical qPCR replicates are not biological replicates.
- Existing samples should have sufficient independent biological replication.
- Any new vertebrate work requires the PI's ethics and experimental review.
- Avoid reproducing an OA exposure merely to obtain a nominally similar
  condition; a sharply defined, feasible mechanistic question is preferable.

## Questions for the PI

1. Are existing zebrafish RNA/cDNA samples available from a relevant stress,
   neural, metabolic, ion-regulation, or developmental model?
2. Which tissues and developmental stages have independent biological
   replicates?
3. Is strand-specific RT-qPCR, ddPCR, RACE, or targeted long-read sequencing
   available?
4. Does the laboratory have a suitable reporter or CRISPRi workflow?
5. What evidence would the PI require before committing laboratory resources?
6. Who should lead assay design and be included in authorship discussions?

## Suggested message

> I am developing a paper from public multi-tissue RNA-seq data in a coral reef
> fish, testing whether transposable-element expression changes during
> developmental and intergenerational exposure to elevated CO2. The original
> material is unavailable, so I do not want to describe zebrafish work as
> direct replication. After the full computational analysis, would you be
> willing to discuss a small cross-species follow-up using existing zebrafish
> material—initially strand-aware RT-qPCR or ddPCR for a few sequence-supported
> candidates? I would bring the effect sizes, family consistency, mapping
> evidence, zebrafish sequence/synteny analysis, and a proposed minimal assay
> before asking for laboratory work.
